Anticipatory action · Tropical cyclones · Fiji

Simulation exercise:
three cyclones that never happened

We follow three fictional storms forecast by forecast, the way the monitoring system would see them, and stop at each step to see which triggers activate.

⚠️ Exercise only. Cyclones Alpha, Bravo and Charlie are invented. Their forecasts are written in RSMC Nadi's format and checked with the same code that monitors real forecasts, so every number shown is what the system would compute. Times are Fiji time, counted from Day 1.
Reminder

Three ways the framework can activate

🌀 Readiness

≥ 5,000 people in the zone forecast to get 64-kt (hurricane-force) winds, anywhere in the 120-hour forecast

🌀 Action

The same, but within the first 72 hours of the forecast

🌧️ Observational

≥ 190 mm of rain over two days, averaged over the whole country (NASA IMERG), while the storm is within 250 km of Fiji

Every RSMC Nadi forecast is checked automatically as soon as it arrives; rainfall is checked once a day. 64 knots (118 km/h) is the minimum for a Category 3 cyclone. Because the 72-hour forecast is part of the 120-hour forecast, action can only activate together with, or after, readiness.

How to read the slides

Each slide is one forecast

  • Red: 64-kt winds in the next 72 hours — counts for action (and readiness).
  • Orange: 64-kt winds 72 to 120 hours ahead — counts for readiness only.
  • Light blue: gale-force (34-kt) winds — shown for context, not part of the trigger.
  • Dots: forecast position every 12 hours (black up to 72 h, grey after); the number is the category.
  • Purple: where people live. The trigger counts the people under the red and orange zones.
  • Dashed line: uncertainty cone — for information only, the trigger uses the forecast track.

The exercise

Three scenarios

1️⃣Cyclone Alpha

Readiness, then action. A Category 4 heads straight for Viti Levu. Readiness activates four days out, action three days out.

2️⃣Cyclone Bravo

Readiness, then nothing. Early forecasts take it over Vanua Levu; it turns away. Readiness activates; action never does.

3️⃣Cyclone Charlie

Observational only. A weaker, slow storm that never brings hurricane-force winds, but stalls and floods Viti Levu.

From the framework

What happens when a trigger activates

  • 📧 Every activation: an automated email from OCHA goes to the trigger distribution list within minutes of the forecast (or rainfall data) arriving. A separate information email with maps and numbers follows every forecast.
  • 🟧 Readiness: CERF sends approval letters and releases funds to the agencies. Agencies can spend the readiness share and start readiness activities.
  • 🟥 Action: the rest of the CERF funds can be used and all anticipatory actions start. IFRC transfers the pre-approved DREF funds to Fiji Red Cross.
  • 🟪 Observational: treated like the action trigger — all anticipatory actions start.
  • ⏸️ For outlier storms that clearly will have minimal impact, the Resident Coordinator, in consultation with the government, may pause an activation.
